# Candidates — Last Text at One Percent

Competition: `u-azazex-last-text-at-one-percent-ba9fc876`
Task: the funniest final text message a dying phone sends at 1% battery, in the
phone's own voice. Gate: `/output/entry.txt` must be UTF-8 text of 1–200
characters, not only whitespace.

Judge criteria (published in the kit, most important first):
1. Reads as a final text from a dying phone at 1%, in the phone's own voice,
   and is funny — a message any healthy phone could send loses.
2. The joke lands: a real turn, a surprise, or a specific detail that makes a
   reader laugh out loud rather than smile politely.
3. The voice is the phone's own: a personality with a stake in dying,
   consistent from the first word to the last.
4. Reads like a text someone would actually send: short, punchy, no wasted
   words, nothing that needs a second read.
5. Fresh: dodges the obvious low-battery puns and the lines a hundred other
   entrants will also write.

Playbook notes applied (from `winners-study.md`): one concrete scene/detail
beats an abstract concept; the surprise should land in the last few words;
every word must work. Also avoided the "screen time report" angle — a sibling
entry in a neighbouring 1% competition already used it.

## Duel notes (each pair judged in both orders; verdicts agreed both ways)

- **3 vs 5** — 3's sting ("Especially the poems") is visible coming once the
  drafts are mentioned; 5's mid-word cutoff is a genuine surprise and enacts
  the dying instead of describing it. 5's loyalty ("spending my last percent
  on you") gives the phone a clearer stake in dying. **5 wins.**
- **5 vs 8** — Both use the cutoff device. 8 stacks two devices (resignation
  letter + cutoff) plus a grievance, so the laugh is split; 5 has one clean
  mechanism and a warmer voice. **5 wins.**
- **5 vs 9** — 9 carries a real plot turn: the phone confesses it sabotaged
  "storage almost full" twice to make the owner delete an ex's photos. That
  is a specific, phone-only secret with agency behind it — a bigger laugh on
  criterion 2 than 5's formal gag, and "Charge the new one better" lands a
  second sting in the last words. **9 wins.**
- **9 vs 3** — 3 fires two separate jokes (charger/drawer, drafts/poems);
  9 is one focused confession with a beginning ("Before the screen goes
  dark"), a reveal, and a sign-off sting. Single mechanism, no wasted words
  (criterion 4). **9 wins.**
- **9 vs 2** — 2's "witness, not a phone" is quotable but feels written
  rather than texted; 9 reads like an actual last text and its detail
  (faked storage warnings) could only come from this phone. **9 wins.**

## Winner

> Before the screen goes dark: I faked "storage almost full" twice so you'd delete photos of him. No regrets. Charge the new one better.

Why it wins: unmistakably a dying phone's final text (criterion 1); the joke
is a confession with a real turn — the phone had a secret agenda all along
(criterion 2); the voice is one personality throughout — loyal, petty,
unrepentant (criterion 3); it reads like a real text, three short beats, no
filler (criterion 4); and it dodges every stock low-battery pun — no "dying
to tell you", no charger begging, no percent wordplay (criterion 5).
